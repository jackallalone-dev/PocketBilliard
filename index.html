    01

    Find your angle

    Tap the cloth to aim at a point, or drag anywhere on the table to swing the cue around the cue ball. Roll the fine-aim wheel for tiny adjustments. The white line shows your ball’s path. A pulsing white ring briefly marks the ball you must hit first; a red “no” sign means you’re aiming at a ball you can’t hit first.

    02

    Pull, then release

    Pull the power cue down (or right on the bottom bar); a full pull is 100%. Release to shoot. Return to the starting point or slide sideways to cancel. Settings swaps the power and aim sides for left or right hands.

    03

    Choose your rules

    Regular 8-ball and 9-ball need no pocket calls. Choose Call Pocket on the menu, then 8-ball or 9-ball, to require ball-and-pocket calls after the break. Make your call to keep shooting, or declare Safety to pass the turn.

    Game flow: Player 1 breaks the first rack and the break alternates after that. Drag the cue ball anywhere behind the head string, then aim and shoot—there is nothing to confirm. Every break stands: pot a ball to keep shooting, otherwise the turn passes. Any foul gives the opponent ball in hand anywhere on the table: drag the cue ball (or its handle) and shoot when ready.
    8-ball fouls (UPA-based): hit a legal ball first, then reach a rail or pocket. Scratching, missing, or hitting the wrong ball first is a foul. An 8 on a legal break wins; an 8 with a foul, early, or off the table loses. Scratching while shooting at the 8 only loses if the 8 also falls.
    Close cue/object-ball shots require a 45° approach under UPA’s double-hit rule. Ordinary fouls do not respot object balls. Safety ends your turn even if balls drop.
    Rules source: UPA Official 8-Ball Rules. Call Pocket 8-ball uses explicit calls. Regular 8-ball is a no-call house variant: the first legally pocketed solid or stripe after the break assigns groups, and a legal 8 in any pocket wins once your group is cleared. Breaks alternate instead of a physical lag, and a dry break is never re-racked. The pro-only three-foul loss rule, optional referee shot clock, and physical conduct/equipment rules are not enabled.
    9-ball: hit the lowest numbered ball (the ringed target) first. A legal 9-ball pocket wins, including on the break or in a combination. No pockets need to be called. Every break stands; a dry break passes the turn. Fouls give ball in hand anywhere; an illegally pocketed or off-table 9 is re-spotted.
    Call Pocket 9-ball: a house variant with the same lowest-first rule. Call any remaining ball and its pocket, including the 9 for a combination. A missed call passes the turn without a foul if the shot is otherwise legal; other balls stay down. The 9 only wins in its called pocket and is otherwise re-spotted. No calls are needed on the break or a declared push-out.
    Push-out: only on the shot immediately after a legal break, declare Push before shooting. A push needs no object contact or rail, but scratches and other fouls still count. A pushed 9 is re-spotted. The opponent chooses to take the next shot or pass it back. Three consecutive fouls lose in 9-ball only if the second-foul warning was given; the warning dialog lets the opponent acknowledge it. Source: UPA Official 9-Ball Rules.
    Playground: choose Playground from the main menu. In Arrange, drag a ball or its offset handle, or use ＋ Balls to select a ball to place. Switch to Shoot to practice without turns or 8-ball penalties. Retry setup restores your practice arrangement. Save setup is available in both phases, with five named slots to save, load, replace, or delete. These slots stay in this browser after reloading. Open the main menu from ☰ → Main menu or by tapping the Pocket Club logo.
    Cue-ball spin: tap the small cue ball, then tap or drag on the big ball where the tip should strike. Below center draws, above center follows, left/right adds sidespin. Draw needs backspin remaining when the cue ball hits another ball; longer distances and softer shots wear it off.
    Cue elevation / jump shots: tap the angle button (0°) and move the slider. Raise the cue butt up to 90° (vertical); the minimum is calculated from the actual height and distance of balls blocking the stick. Lowest selects that minimum. Angle, contact point, and power determine spin, hop height, and reach. The amber jump guide or violet swerve/massé guide follows the simulated shot (65% power while idle). Balls can land and roll on the rail, return to the cloth, or fall into a pocket. Leaving the outer edge or stopping on top of the rail is out of bounds; normal match fouls still apply.
