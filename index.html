    01

    Find your angle

    Tap the cloth to aim at a point, or grab and drag the stick to rotate it around the cue ball. Roll the FINE AIM wheel for tiny adjustments. The white line shows your ball’s path. A pulsing white ring briefly marks the ball you must hit first; a red “no” sign means you’re aiming at a ball you can’t hit first.

    02

    Pull, then release

    Follow the power control’s arrow: pull down beside the table, or right on the bottom bar. Halfway is 100%, and a full pull is 200%. Release to shoot. Return to the starting point or move across the pull direction to cancel. Settings mirrors the controls for left or right hands.

    03

    Choose your rules

    Regular 8-ball and 9-ball need no pocket calls. Choose Call Pocket on the menu, then 8-ball or 9-ball, to require ball-and-pocket calls after the break. Make your call to keep shooting, or declare Safety to pass the turn.

    UPA amateur rules: a legal break pockets a numbered ball or sends four distinct object balls to rails. A legal 8-ball break wins; an 8-ball break with a foul loses. Fouls give the opponent ball in hand—behind the head string after a break foul, anywhere otherwise. Hit a legal ball first, then reach a rail or pocket. Scratching while shooting at the 8 only loses if the 8 also falls.
    Use the move handle to place the cue, then tap ✓ Ready. After a break foul, the first object ball hit must be outside the kitchen. Close cue/object-ball shots require a 45° approach under UPA’s double-hit rule. Ordinary fouls do not respot object balls. Safety ends your turn even if balls drop.
    Rules source: UPA Official 8-Ball Rules. Call Pocket 8-ball uses explicit calls. Regular 8-ball is a no-call house variant: the first legally pocketed solid or stripe after the break assigns groups, and a legal 8 in any pocket wins once your group is cleared. The first breaker is selected instead of a physical lag; subsequent racks default to alternating breaks. The pro-only three-foul loss rule, optional referee shot clock, and physical conduct/equipment rules are not enabled.
    9-ball: hit the lowest numbered ball (the ringed target) first. A legal 9-ball pocket wins, including on the break or in a combination. No pockets need to be called. A legal break pockets a ball or sends three object balls to rails. Fouls give ball in hand anywhere; an illegally pocketed or off-table 9 is re-spotted.
    Call Pocket 9-ball: a house variant with the same lowest-first rule. Call any remaining ball and its pocket, including the 9 for a combination. A missed call passes the turn without a foul if the shot is otherwise legal; other balls stay down. The 9 only wins in its called pocket and is otherwise re-spotted. No calls are needed on the break or a declared push-out.
    Push-out: only on the shot immediately after a legal break, declare Push before shooting. A push needs no object contact or rail, but scratches and other fouls still count. A pushed 9 is re-spotted. The opponent chooses to take the next shot or pass it back. Three consecutive fouls lose in 9-ball only if the second-foul warning was given; the warning dialog lets the opponent acknowledge it. Source: UPA Official 9-Ball Rules.
    Playground: choose Playground from the main menu. In Arrange, drag a ball or its offset handle, or use ＋ Balls to select a ball to place. Switch to Shoot to practice without turns or 8-ball penalties. Retry setup restores your practice arrangement. Save setup is available in both phases, with five named slots to save, load, replace, or delete. These slots stay in this browser after reloading. Open the main menu from ☰ → Main menu or by tapping the Pocket Club logo.
    Cue-ball spin: tap SPIN above the power control. Hit below center for draw, above center for follow, or left/right for sidespin. Draw needs backspin remaining when the cue ball hits another ball; longer distances and softer shots wear it off.
    Cue elevation / jump shots: open ANGLE and move the Vertical cue angle slider. Raise the cue butt up to 90° (vertical); the minimum is calculated from the actual height and distance of balls blocking the stick. Auto clearance selects that minimum. Angle, contact point, and power determine spin, hop height, and reach. The amber jump guide or violet swerve/massé guide follows the simulated shot (65% power while idle). Balls can land and roll on the rail, return to the cloth, or fall into a pocket. Leaving the outer edge or stopping on top of the rail is out of bounds; normal match fouls still apply.
